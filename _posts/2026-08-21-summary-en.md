### [Largest 2D Map of the Universe Released](https://newscenter.lbl.gov/2026/08/10/scientists-release-biggest-2d-map-of-the-universe/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Scientists have released the most comprehensive 2D map of the universe, based on data from the Legacy Survey. The map is accessible via an interactive viewer at viewer.legacysurvey.org, allowing users to explore the sky in detail. This release provides a vast dataset for astronomical research, covering a significant portion of the sky with high precision. The map is expected to remain the most detailed 2D map for years to come, serving as a key resource for studying galaxies, dark matter, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

hackernews · NKosmatos · Aug 21, 18:36 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49392200)

**「Background」** The DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys is a project that combines imaging data from three ground-based telescopes—the Dark Energy Camera Legacy Survey \(DECaLS\), the Beijing-Arizona Sky Survey \(BASS\), and the Mayall z-band Legacy Survey \(MzLS\)—to create a comprehensive survey of the sky. This effort is part of the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument \(DESI\) project, which aims to study dark energy by mapping the universe in three dimensions. The newly released 2D map, containing 5.6 trillion pixels and nearly 4 billion celestial objects, serves as the foundation for the largest-ever 3D map of the universe, which will be used to investigate the nature of dark energy.

**「Impact」** Astronomers and researchers will benefit from an unprecedented dataset for studying galaxy distribution and cosmology, while the public gains access to an interactive tool for exploring the universe. The map&\#x27;s longevity suggests it will underpin many future studies, though its dominance may be challenged by upcoming 3D surveys.

**「Community Discussion」** Commenters expressed awe at the map&\#x27;s detail, with some noting that closer inspection reveals more galaxies in seemingly empty regions. Others questioned the feasibility of extending the map to 3D, citing computational challenges, while a few speculated about future investment in astronomy given economic and geopolitical factors.
